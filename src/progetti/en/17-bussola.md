Bussola puts the countries of the world on the "political compass", but in three dimensions: state economy or free market, authoritarian or libertarian, nationalist or internationalist. Each country is a point in a 3D space you can rotate, and a timeline lets you follow its path year by year, from 1900 to today.

The axes can be changed (openness to the world, secularism, inequality, education, corruption and more) and the site calculates the correlations between them. Countries can be coloured by continent, alliance, political regime, religion or income; you can isolate groups such as the EU, NATO, BRICS or the G7 with their average and the axis that best describes them, or switch on the trail of historical governments (Mussolini, Stalin, Franco, Pinochet...) for the years they were in power.

The data comes from public academic sources (V-Dem, V-Party, KOF, Correlates of War, the World Bank), processed in Python and normalized on a scale from -10 to +10; for every value the site shows the source and the raw figure. The interface is available in six languages.
