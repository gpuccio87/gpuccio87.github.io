Hundreds of articles come out every day, and many of them tell the same story. Agencies, newspapers, websites: the same piece of news bounces around ten, twenty times, and meanwhile the important stories risk ending up buried under the newest ones.

That's where **Sieve** comes from: I wanted something that would read the news for me and tell me which stories really matter.

### From five hundred articles to a few dozen facts

Every two hours Sieve gathers articles from about **ninety sources**: news agencies, Italian newspapers and international outlets. Articles about the same event become **a single fact**, with all its sources.

A typical day means about 500 articles, which become 460 facts. Of those, only a few dozen clear the threshold.

### A weight, not a timestamp

Every fact gets a **weight from 0 to 10** that measures how much it matters, not how recent it is. At the top of the page you'll find what carries weight, with a short summary and links to the original articles.

You choose the threshold: a chart shows how the day's weights are distributed, and you decide how fine the sieve should be. You can read it as a list or as a newspaper page, and filter by section.

### Something to listen to, too

Twice a day, morning and evening, Sieve becomes a **podcast** a few minutes long with the main facts. You can listen on the site, in any podcast app through the RSS feed, or in the **Telegram** group, which also gets the news roundup.

### No catches

No paywall, no ads, no cookies. The summaries are automatic, but the original article is always one click away. Sieve is free and runs on donations from the people who find it useful.

You'll find it at [sieve.gabrielepucciarelli.com](https://sieve.gabrielepucciarelli.com) (the site is in Italian): try it for a few days and tell me whether the sieve works.
